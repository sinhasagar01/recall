The rules

Every decision this mock encodes, in one place. Where this page and the screens disagree, the screens win.

Data
  • One table, two shapesThree columns on topics: kind ('topic' | 'quiz'), options text[], correct_option int. No second table, no quiz history, no scores.
  • The constraints are the featureA quiz requires 2+ options and a correct_option that indexes into them. A topic requires both to be null. Enforced in the database, not just the form.
  • Why reuses mental_modelIt does the same job — not what the answer is, but why. One field, one register, one voice across both shapes.
  • Quizzes are independentNot generated from topics, not attached to them. Interview questions and "what does this print", not concepts already saved.
Behaviour
  • Select, then CheckA tap never writes. The database write happens on Check, so a mis-tap on a phone cannot permanently mark something weak.
  • Grading is objectiveCorrect → strong, incorrect → weak. There is no 'okay' for a quiz, and no self-assessment — this is the one thing quizzes do better than topics.
  • Only two options get markedThe correct one, and your pick if it was wrong. Everything else goes grey.
  • Options shuffle per sessionUsing the existing seeded shuffle. Otherwise by the third round you are recalling "the second one".
  • Options stop being buttons after CheckSame shape so nothing shifts, but no hover, not focusable, not announced as actionable.
  • Separate practice/practice?scope=quiz, following the existing scope pattern. Same session size, same floor rules.
Shared, unchanged
  • Confidence, weak page, orderingIdentical for both shapes. A weak quiz and a weak topic sit in the same list, ordered by the same rule.
  • SearchMatches a quiz's question, its options and its explanation, alongside everything it already matches.
  • Colour is never the only signalEvery marked option carries a shape and a text tag. Readable in greyscale, announced via aria-live.
  • ExportQuizzes appear in both library.json and library.md.

Explicitly not built: quiz history, scores, streaks, analytics, timers, AI-generated questions, AI grading, and images on quizzes. A quiz's entire record is its confidence and its practice count, exactly like a topic's.
