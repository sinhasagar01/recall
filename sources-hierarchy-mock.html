Two counts that mean different things

3 of 5 mined is lessons that produced something. 1h 04m is the length of the lessons you added. A course that is 8 of 42 lessons might be a third of the hours or a twentieth — the count flatters and the duration does not.

Corrected: four things this screen drew that it should not have

The progress bars are gone. They were drawn at 62% and 50% beside “3 of 5 mined” and “1 of 2 mined” — 62% is not 3 of 5, and more importantly a bar needs a denominator. The app knows how many lessons you have added, never how many the course has. The rules tab of this very file says so: “pretending to would be the first fake number in this app.”

“2 courses” counted the absence of a course as a course. One of the two groups is “No course”. The head now reads “1 course · 2 without one”.

“4h 12m watched” claimed something the app cannot know. It knows the lengths you typed, not what you watched. Now “4h 12m of lessons”.

Lessons now read oldest-first. They were drawn newest-first, while this file's own principle is that the list reads “the way the course sidebar reads” — and a sidebar is syllabus order. You add lessons as you watch them, so ascending IS course order, and the next one to watch is at the bottom. Courses and chapters stay newest-first: those are navigation, this is a syllabus.

Lessons with no course group under "No course" rather than being hidden or forced into one. A one-off conference talk is a real source and should not need a course invented for it.

Corrected: one dot, not five

This screen drew arc 2's five extraction dots against every lesson. Arc 6 deleted that checklist — it was a proxy for "have I mined this video", and extraction made the real answer cheap enough to show directly. There is nothing left to count to five.

One dot now, and it means finished: every entry this lesson produced is okay or better. Green because it is derived and binary, which is the whole of the --ok rule. The build was already right about this; the drawing was stale.

The thread of execution

JavaScriptexecution
Definition

The interpreter goes through code line by line, executing each one and saving any data or functions it meets into memory as it goes.

Mental model

Map it down the blackboard — a thread going down the page, doing each line as it reaches it.

Where this came from
Source JavaScript: The Hard Parts, v3 › Principles of JavaScript › Execution Context 13m 23s · 3 other entries

Every level is a link. The course opens the course group, the chapter opens the chapter, the lesson opens its workspace. That is what "I know exactly where it came from" means in practice — not a note, a route back.

The rules

A change to arc 2's sources, not a new arc. One migration, one form, one grouped list.

Fields
  • Title becomes LessonA source is a lesson. The old form said "Title" with an unexplained "Course" beneath it, which is why neither was clear. Rename in the schema too — sources.title → sources.lesson.
  • Chapter is new, and optionalBetween course and lesson, matching how a course is actually laid out.
  • Order is course → chapter → lesson, top to bottomBroadest first, the way the sidebar of any course reads.
  • Length is new, and optionalFree text. Stored as seconds so it can be summed, entered however you like.
  • Only the lesson is requiredUnchanged.
Entry
  • Course and chapter autocomplete from your own dataCourse offers every course you have used, with a lesson count. Chapter offers only chapters already in the selected course. Both accept a new value — the list is a convenience, not a constraint.
  • Save and add the next lessonKeeps course and chapter, clears lesson, URL, length and transcript, focuses the lesson field. Ten lessons from one chapter in a sitting is the real workflow.
  • A live breadcrumb above SaveShows exactly what will be stored, so an empty chapter or a mistyped course is visible before you press.
The list
  • Grouped course → chapter → lesson, collapsibleA flat list is unreadable after two courses.
  • Lessons with no course group under "No course"A conference talk is a real source and should not need a course invented for it. Same for a lesson with no chapter inside a course.
  • Two counts, because they say different things"3 of 5 mined" is lessons that produced something; "1h 04m" is time actually spent. A count flatters, a duration does not.
  • Practise this chapter and Practise this courseThe ?scope=source pattern one and two levels up, resolved to topic ids outside the queue exactly as arc 6 does — the queue still cannot see a source.
Decided against
  • No courses or chapters tableTwo text columns with autocomplete, not two entities. A chapter has no properties of its own, nothing links to it, and a table would need its own RLS, policies, cascades and pgTAP for a string.
  • No starts_at offsetThe rule is one lesson per source, so extraction timestamps are already relative to the right thing. If you ever paste a whole chapter, that is when this gets revisited.
  • No progress percentage over a course"3 of 5 mined" counts what you have; it does not know the course has 42 lessons, and pretending to would be the first fake number in this app. The list tab drew a bar at 62% anyway — this rule and that drawing could not both stand, and the drawing lost.
