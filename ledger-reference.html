There is no ledger item detail page

A row is the item. The title links straight out to where the thing actually lives — a PR, a markdown ADR in the repo, an Excalidraw board, a GitHub issue. Building a page to display a title and a URL would be a place to look at a link instead of following it, and storing the ADR's context, alternatives and consequences would make this a document store, which is the decision already taken against.

Newest first, one list, filter chips with counts. No board, no columns, no drag, no burn-down. The only thing this page knows that a folder of links does not is which capability an item serves — and that is the whole reason it is in Recall rather than in a bookmark bar.

Adding an item

One small form. Everything except the title and the kind is optional, because a decision you have made but not written up yet is still a decision.

Kind
Status

Three states, and the words change with the kind: an ADR is Draft · Decided · Superseded, a task is Open · Done · Dropped, an incident is Open · Closed · Dropped. Corrected: this form showed “Open” for an ADR while the States tab defined it as “Draft”. The States tab is the definition, and the form renders the selected kind's own vocabulary.

Serves optional · a capability

No date field — the item is stamped when you add it, and back-dating a decision you made last week is the kind of tidying that turns a record into a story. No ADR body, no alternatives, no consequences: those live at the link, in a file you can diff, which is what an ADR is for.

The ledger line does not change whether a capability is demonstrated

Demonstrated is still recall plus evidence, exactly as arc 3 defined it. A ledger item is a link to something outside the app — the product cannot know whether you actually shipped it, and a rule that trusted a pasted URL would be a rule you could satisfy with a bookmark. The line is context, not a third half.

A capability with no ledger items shows no ledger line at all, the rule every section on these pages follows. The count links to the ledger filtered to that capability — the one filter this arc adds, because it is the reason an item knows about a capability in the first place.

Why a sheet and not a fourth tab

DESIGN.md rule 13 — mobile has two destinations plus add — survives untouched, because none of these six is a destination you bounce between. They are places you go deliberately, once. The sheet reuses the existing focus trap and Escape handling, carries the same counts the rail does, and puts Delete account at the bottom in --flag, which is the one place it belongs.

This replaces the wrapping cluster shipped in arc 3. That row was correct at five and this is what it becomes at six — the arc 3 measurement is the evidence, not a change of mind: five inline overflowed by 51px, five wrapped cost a second line, and six would wrap to three.

The rules

Every decision this reference encodes. Where this page and the screens disagree, the screens win.

Data
  • One new table, project_itemsKind and title required; link, note, status and capability optional. Nullable capability_id with on delete set null, the same shape as source_id and topics.capability_id.
  • Six kinds, three statusesKinds: adr, task, diagram, incident, milestone, scale_exercise. Statuses: open, settled, retired — one stored enum, rendered in each kind's own vocabulary. Six statuses would be a workflow.
  • No documents, everNo ADR body, context, alternatives or consequences. No file upload, no image storage. The link is the integration, and the thing lives where you made it.
  • No detail pageA row is the item; the title opens the link. A page to display a title and a URL is a place to look at a link instead of following it.
  • "Superseded by" is free textNot a self-referencing FK. One line of prose does not justify a graph.
Behaviour
  • The ledger never changes whether a capability is demonstratedDemonstrated stays recall plus evidence, exactly as arc 3 defined it. The product cannot know whether you shipped what a URL points at, and a rule satisfiable with a bookmark is not a rule. The ledger line on a capability is context.
  • Ledger items are never practisedNo confidence, never in the queue or on the weak page. Asserted the way arcs 1, 2 and 3 assert their boundaries — four arcs, one boundary, four directions.
  • Stamped when added, never back-datedNo date field. Back-dating a decision you made last week turns a record into a story.
  • Superseded rows stayIn place, at their original date, in grey. A ledger that deletes what you changed your mind about is not a record.
  • The delete confirmation says what is not deleted"Nothing at the link is touched — the ADR stays in your repo."
  • Status is drawn in ink, never green — correctedAn earlier draft of this page painted the settled status in --ok. DESIGN.md reserves that colour for what the app derives, and a ledger status is something you set by hand — this page's own callout argues the product cannot know whether you shipped what a URL points at. The three states are told apart by shape and weight: dashed hollow, filled ink, hollow grey. Four references running have reached for a colour that means “good” or “bad” without checking what the token means; the check is to ask what kind of claim the thing makes, not how it should feel.
